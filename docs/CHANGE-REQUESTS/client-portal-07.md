# client-portal-07 — the shared-zone changes the marketing switch, phone notifications and the Send screen need

**Status.** For the integrator, 2026-10-06. Round 75 (`round-75/marketing-and-push`) builds steps 3, 4 and 5 of the push memo (`docs/OPERATOR/2026-09-17-push-notifications.md`; the operator answered all four decisions "as recommended" on 6 October 2026, and approved the marketing wording drafted in round 72 the same day). Everything below is outside the client-portal stream's own paths and rides in the round's own pull request, as client-portal-06 carried the announcements'. Each edit is additive and breaks no caller, except item 6, which mends one.

**What, and why.**

1. **The approved wording.** `docs/CONSENT/marketing.en.md` and `marketing.ar.md` move up from `drafts/` at `1.0`, `status: approved`, `approved: 2026-10-06`; the draft notice at the head of each is the only text removed. `docs/CONSENT/drafts/README.md` (new) keeps the folder and says what it is for; `docs/CONSENT/README.md` gains the fifth purpose and a section on how this one is given. The pins move with it: `db/seed/consent-text.test.ts` (five purposes, ten texts, `marketing` no longer retired) and `tests/db/consent-text.test.ts` (ten seeded wordings). `tests/portal/consent-drafts.test.ts` (the portal's own) keeps guarding the drafts folder.
2. **`consent_method` gains `portal_switch`** (migration 706, in the portal's range). `tests/db/schema.test.ts` pins the enum's four values; `docs/SPEC/00-data-model.md` names the value under `consent`.
3. **`docs/SPEC/00-data-model.md`**: `push_subscription`, `push_message` and `push_recipient` join section 3 beside `announcement` (migration 707).
4. **`domain/shared/actor.ts`** and its test: `portal.push.send`, for the owner and an admin — decision 3's "written by you or an admin, in Settings … and sent from there".
5. **`domain/shared/audit-narrative.ts`** and its test: three entity words and sentences in both languages for a notification sent (an offer named as one), delivered, its recipients recorded and read, a phone turned on and off and leaving, and offers turned on and off on the portal; the method word for `portal_switch`.
6. **`app/api/clients/record-schema.ts`** and **`app/admin/clients/ConsentTab.tsx`**: the client record's answer now reads every method a consent may carry (`RECORDED_CONSENT_METHODS`), and the Consent tab labels the switch "Turned on by the person, on their portal". Without it, the practice's own record of any client whose adult turned offers on answered 500 (`tests/portal/db/marketing_routes.test.ts` proves both: the record reads, and the console's recording forms still refuse `portal_switch`).
7. **`app/api/create-api.ts`** and **`app/api/server.ts`**: `ApiOptions.push`, the phone notifications' sender, passed to `mountPortal`; the server builds it from the three `PUSH_VAPID_*` variables (`pushSenderFromEnv`), off cleanly when none is set, and says which at startup.
8. **`app/shell/sw.ts`** and its test: the `push` and `notificationclick` listeners. Nothing is cached; a window is opened only on a portal path of this origin.
9. **`app/shell/App.tsx`** and `App.test.tsx`, **`app/shell/adminAccess.ts`** (`canOpenNotifications`), **`app/shell/AdminLayout.tsx`** and `AdminLayout.test.ts`, **`app/shell/components/Rail.tsx`**, **`app/admin/settings/SettingsNav.tsx`**: the route `/admin/settings/notifications`, lazily loaded, behind `canOpenNotifications`; the rail's Settings family and the strip list it for the owner and an admin. The screen itself is the portal stream's (`app/admin/portal/NotificationsPage.tsx`), and its Arabic boxes and preview are the announcements' allow-listed `AnnouncementArabic.tsx`, so `tests/lint/console-is-english.test.ts` is unchanged.
10. **`docs/COMPLIANCE/approved-vendors.md`**: the row for Apple's, Google's and Mozilla's push services — a device address and a sealed message they cannot read — approved by the operator's "as recommended" on decision 2. (In this branch's history the row's commit follows the sender's by a few commits; both land in one pull request, and nothing sends until the operator sets the key pair.)
11. **`scripts/push-keys.mjs`** (new) with its types, **`package.json`** (`pnpm push:keys`), **`.env.example`** (the three variables, empty): the operator's one act at deploy time.

**No new dependency.** Web push is RFC 8291 and 8292 written with Node's own crypto in `app/api/portal/push/webpush.ts`, proved byte for byte against RFC 8291's worked example. The `web-push` package was not taken: it would have brought several transitive packages to the host's scanner for about a hundred lines.

12. **Core migration `975_push_devices_redacted_and_erased.sql`** (trunk range, taken into this round on the coordinator's word of 6 October 2026; 974 is reserved for the helper-role stream). It does two things, each a whole restatement of the latest definition on main with one addition:
    - `app.audit_redact`, from 967: it now drops `push_endpoint`, `push_p256dh` and `push_auth`, so a device's address and keys never reach the append-only trail.
    - `app.erase_client`, from 972: a new step 2a deletes the `push_subscription` rows of exactly the household accounts the erasure closes. That is the accounts step 2 archives, so a spared colleague's devices stand (968).

    `tests/portal/db/push.test.ts` proves both, and fails without the migration. The erasure's summary is unchanged.

**Recorded follow-up, not built.** Sign-out on a shared device: the shell's sign-out could also let this device's push subscription go, as the portal's own "turn off" already does. Until then, a shared tablet keeps receiving the practice's news and, if the last person's switch is on, its offers; neither carries personal data. This lasts until the next person turns notifications on with that tablet, which moves the device to them.
